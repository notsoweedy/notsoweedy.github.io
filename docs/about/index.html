About me

I’m Vidhi, a software engineer at Microsoft in Hyderabad. I work on the infrastructure that lets engineers test pre-production hardware (chips from NVIDIA, Qualcomm, Intel and AMD) before it ships. Before that I spent four years at VMware building the tools admins use to install, update and recover vCenter.

What pulls me in

I like the layer between hardware and the people using it. Most of my best work there has been about recovery: putting a device back to a known state, rolling back a failed update, or tracing a crash dump to a root cause. Good infrastructure is invisible, and I find that strangely satisfying.

Recently that interest has gone one level lower, toward systems for machine learning:

  • how GPUs manage memory, and what unified virtual memory really costs
  • why launching a kernel is expensive, and how CUDA Graphs batch launches to cut that cost
  • how to keep expensive accelerators busy instead of waiting

At work I’ve validated ML models across CPU, GPU and NPU execution providers on unreleased silicon. Now I want to understand the systems underneath properly, and build some of them. There’s more on the Now page.

How I work

  • If I can’t explain something simply, I don’t understand it yet. Writing it down is how I find out.
  • I like turning one-off hacks into platforms. ALIS started as a hackathon project and now manages 150+ devices.
  • I care about the boring details: accessibility, security, rollback paths.

Off the clock

I’m a markets nerd. I’ve taken courses in quantitative modeling (UPenn) and technical analysis (NYIF), and I’m building a swing-trading assistant for Indian equities that never places an order until I approve it. I also end up at a lot of hackathons.

School

B.Tech in Information Technology from Manipal Institute of Technology (2016 to 2020). During it I spent a winter as an academic intern at the National University of Singapore. The details are under Work and school.
